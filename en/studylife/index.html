Privacy policy for the StudyLife app

Version 1.0 · Last updated: 14 November 2026

In short. StudyLife is an app for self-hosted server software for planning and tracking your studies. The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting software and sends your data only to the StudyLife server whose address you enter, and to Apple for notifications. The operator of that server, not the publisher of the app, is responsible for the data stored there. Data from Apple Health is evaluated on your device only and never leaves it.

2. Scope: who is responsible for what

StudyLife is open-source software (github.com/lukislp/studylife) made of a server that each person or organisation runs themselves and the app that connects to such a server. This policy applies to the versions of the app distributed through Apple's App Store for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. Responsibilities differ:

  1. The app as published by the publisher on the App Store. It processes data mostly on your device and uses Apple services (App Store, notification service, Apple Health, speech recognition). The publisher receives no personal data from you through the app, with the exceptions in sections 3.10 (marketplace via GitHub) and 3.11 (voluntary crash reports via Apple). See section 3.
  2. Self-hosted StudyLife servers. Whoever runs the server you connect the app to is responsible for your account and all content stored there (for example study sessions, notes, courses, goals and grades) and must provide you with their own privacy policy. The publisher has no access to these servers. See section 5.
  3. The public demo at studylife-demo.lktec.org, run by the publisher. See section 6.
  4. Optional AI features, which only a server's operator can set up and which send data to an external provider only after your consent. See section 7.
  5. Payments do not exist in StudyLife: the app offers no purchases.

3. The app on your device

3.1 Download from the App Store

The app is distributed through Apple's App Store. When you download it, Apple (Apple Distribution International Ltd., Ireland, or Apple Inc., USA) processes data such as your Apple ID, device data and purchase information under its own responsibility. The publisher has no influence on this. For details, see Apple's privacy policy.

3.2 What the app connects to

On first launch you enter the address of your StudyLife server. There is no default address. After that, the app connects only to

  • the StudyLife server whose address you entered,
  • the Apple Push Notification service for notifications (section 3.4), and
  • GitHub's programming interface, when you open the marketplace for add-ons (section 3.10).

The app contains no third-party software for audience measurement, advertising, tracking or crash reporting and uses neither Firebase nor any comparable service. Links, for example to the self-hosting guide or to "Report a problem" (a prefilled form on GitHub with the app version, platform, browser family and language), open in your browser; nothing is sent until you submit the form there yourself, and then GitHub's privacy terms apply.

You sign in with a passkey on a sign-in page of your server, opened in a system window. There are no passwords. Confirmation with Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode is handled by the operating system; the app receives no biometric data.

3.4 Notifications (push and local)

The app asks for permission to send you notifications. Reminders that the app schedules itself are created on the device. For notifications from your server, the app receives a device token from Apple and sends it, with the device name iOS gives the app (usually the model name such as "iPhone"), to your StudyLife server, which stores both. Your server sends notifications and Live Activity updates through the Apple Push Notification service (APNs) of Apple Inc. Apple processes the device token and the content.

The content can include reminders, reports, nudges and achievements, including course names and topics; Live Activity updates contain the state of the study timer (phase, round, end time). The server's operator is responsible for the processing on the server (section 5). Your server deletes the device token when you disconnect from the server, when Apple reports it as invalid and when your account is deleted. You can turn notifications off at any time in the system settings.

3.5 Apple Health

When you tap "Connect Apple Health" under "Setup" in the app, iOS asks which data you want to share. Before that, the app does not access Apple Health. The app

  • reads heart rate variability (for a readiness tile, from 14 days of data), sleep analysis (sleep regularity over 30 nights), step count (a movement break nudge after about 25 minutes of focus) and VO2 max (cardio fitness trend in the statistics), and
  • writes one "Mindful Minutes" entry for each completed focus round.

The values read are evaluated and shown on your device only. They are not sent to your server, to the publisher or to any third party. Only if you have agreed to telemetry (section 3.8) does the app tell your server whether a query succeeded and whether there was enough data – without values or counts. Apple Health stores the Mindful Minutes written; whether they are synced via iCloud depends on your settings with Apple.

Health data enjoys special protection. Insofar as this involves processing of personal data by the publisher, it is based on your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) and Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG), which you give by granting access in iOS. You can withdraw it at any time, entirely or for individual data types, in the Health app or in Settings under "Privacy & Security → Health". Apple Health is only available on iPhone.

3.6 Dictating notes

To dictate notes, the app records with the microphone and sends the recording to your StudyLife server, which converts it to text there. The operator of your server is responsible for this. If recording is not possible, the app falls back to Apple's speech recognition; Apple then processes the spoken content, depending on device, language and settings, on the device or on Apple's servers under its own responsibility. The microphone is used only while you have actively started a dictation.

3.7 Widgets, Live Activity, Apple Watch, shortcuts and calendar files

Widgets, the study timer's Live Activity, the Apple Watch app and the shortcuts (open the focus timer, ask for the streak, ask for today's or this week's study time, pause or resume the timer) use the summary from section 3.3. These extensions do not open network connections themselves. Pausing or resuming the timer and the quick actions on the app icon are carried out by the app itself (in the background if necessary), which sends the command to your server. If you open a calendar file (.ics) with StudyLife, the app reads the file to add the events to your StudyLife installation. In the background, the app occasionally refreshes its cache from your server.

3.8 Telemetry (only with consent, to your server)

On first launch, StudyLife asks once whether you want to send technical measurements to improve errors and loading times. Without your agreement nothing is sent. With your agreement, the app sends to your own StudyLife server (not to the publisher): loading times, response times and status codes of requests, error kinds with a cleaned, shortened error location (without the error text), the app's memory and data usage, and crash and hang reports that iOS provides to the app via MetricKit. The events carry a random session ID that changes every 24 hours, but no user ID, no IP address and no content. You can withdraw your consent at any time in Setup (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). The operator of your server is responsible for evaluating it.

3.9 Local network

Many StudyLife servers are on a home network, for example on a NAS. iOS therefore asks for permission to reach devices on the local network. The app uses this permission only to reach your StudyLife server.

3.10 Marketplace for add-ons (GitHub)

When you open the marketplace in Setup, the app fetches the public list of add-ons from the repository lukislp/studylife-marketplace through GitHub's programming interface (api.github.com) and caches it for up to 24 hours. As technically necessary, GitHub, Inc. (88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA) receives your IP address, date and time and the app's browser identifier. No content or account data is sent. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in showing you the add-on overview you requested (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitHub processes this data under its privacy statement and, according to GitHub, bases transfers to the USA on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses. If you do not open the marketplace, no connection to GitHub is made.

3.11 Crash reports and usage statistics via Apple

If you have turned on "Share with App Developers" in the system settings under "Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements", Apple provides the publisher with crash reports and aggregated usage statistics for the app. According to Apple, this data is only shared in a form that does not identify you. The publisher uses it only to find and fix errors. The legal basis is the consent you gave to Apple (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw in the system settings at any time. The data is kept in App Store Connect for as long as Apple provides.

5. Self-hosted StudyLife servers

The operator of the server you connect the app to is responsible for all data stored on that server – for example you yourself, a family member, a study group or an institution. Please contact that operator with questions about this data and to exercise your rights. They must provide you with their own privacy policy.

To help you understand what is typically processed there, the overview below describes the features of the software. What a particular server actually stores, for how long and with whom it shares data is decided by its operator.

  • Account: display name and passkeys (public key, device label, timestamps); no email address and no password. Sessions are stored as a hash with timestamps.
  • Content: study sessions (course, times, topic, note), notes, courses and programmes, goals per course including optional grades, settings, timer state and notification subscriptions. If you wish, you can create a public, read-only progress link (ECTS, grade average, topic progress).
  • Add-ons such as webhooks, Telegram, Alexa, Home Assistant, browser extensions or AI (section 7) are each turned on by you; they can send data to the services you choose.
  • Export: you can download your study sessions, notes, goals and settings as a file in the app.

5.1 Deleting your account

You can delete your account directly in the app under "Setup". For security you confirm the deletion with your passkey or a recovery code (in the app also by a sign-in no more than two minutes old); an export is offered first. The server then deletes your account with all content, passkeys, sessions, keys and notification subscriptions and instructs connected add-ons to delete your data as well. Copies in the operator's backups remain until they expire; details are in your operator's privacy policy. For the data on your device, see section 3.3.

6. Public demo (studylife-demo.lktec.org)

At studylife-demo.lktec.org, the publisher runs a demo installation (controller: see section 1) that you can open in a browser or by entering this address in the app. All visitors are signed in automatically to the same demo account with prepared sample data. Changes, registration, account deletion, telemetry, dictation and AI features are switched off there.

6.1 Delivery via Cloudflare and hosting

The demo is reached through the Cloudflare network (Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). Cloudflare accepts every connection, processes as a processor, as technically necessary, your IP address, date and time, the requested address, the browser identifier and connection characteristics, protects the demo against attacks and forwards the request encrypted to the server. Beyond this technically necessary, short-lived processing for security, Cloudflare keeps no access logs and produces no analytics for us; additional features such as Web Analytics or Logpush are not turned on. According to Cloudflare, it bases transfers to the USA on its certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses.

The demo's server is a virtual server at Hostinger International Ltd. (Cyprus), acting as a processor. The server is located in a data centre in Germany; hosting involves no transfer of data to third countries.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in presenting the software publicly and securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The standard data processing agreements of both providers under Art. 28 GDPR apply: for Cloudflare its Data Processing Addendum, which forms part of Cloudflare's terms, and for Hostinger its Data Processing Addendum, which supplements Hostinger's terms of service and covers VPS services.

6.2 What the demo stores about you

The demo stores neither your IP address nor your browser identifier: the web server in front of the demo writes no access log, and StudyLife itself does not log this information. For protective limits, the IP address is used in memory only. For each visit, the demo creates a sign-in session (a hash of the session key and timestamps). These sessions are deleted at the regular reset of the demo, which takes place at least once a week and rebuilds all demo data. In your browser or in the app, the demo stores the session key, caches and the language in local storage (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG); it sets no cookies. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (see above).

7. Optional AI features

The operator of a StudyLife server can set up an AI assistant (the "studylife-ai" add-on) that answers questions about your study content, enriches notes and carries out tasks. Which models are used is decided by the operator alone.

  • Data sent: your notes (title and content), courses (name, code, semester, ECTS, group, topics), study sessions of the last five years (course, times, status, topic, note) and goals (target date, grade, completion date and topics, completion note, tag). This content is regularly transferred to the AI service and converted into vectors there with an embedding model; with every question, your message and matching excerpts go to the language model.
  • Possible recipients: the provider chosen by the operator, for example OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral, Microsoft Azure or another provider connected via LiteLLM, or a locally run model (for example Ollama), in which case no data goes to third parties.
  • Consent: before any data goes to an external provider, StudyLife shows you the provider, the models and the data shared, and asks for your explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). The consent applies only to these providers and models; if the operator changes them, you are asked again. Consent is only not required if the operator declares that all models run locally.
  • Withdrawal and deletion: you can withdraw your consent at any time in Setup. The AI service's access is then revoked and your data there is deleted; the same happens when your account is deleted. The AI service keeps conversation states for up to 30 days. How long the external provider keeps data depends on its terms.
  • Third countries: several of these providers are based in the USA. The transfer, its legal basis and the safeguards are described by the operator in their privacy policy.

The operator of your server is responsible for this processing. The publisher receives none of this data. AI is switched off in the public demo.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to

  • access the data stored about you (Art. 15),
  • rectification of inaccurate data (Art. 16),
  • erasure (Art. 17),
  • restriction of processing (Art. 18),
  • data portability (Art. 20),
  • withdraw consent with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)); this does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before the withdrawal.

Right to object (Art. 21 GDPR). Where we process data on the basis of our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), you can object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. We will then no longer process the data unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds.

For processing by the publisher (sections 3 and 6), contact the email address in section 1. Because the demo stores nothing that would let us recognise you, we usually cannot match a request about a demo visit to any record (Art. 11 GDPR). For data on a self-hosted server, contact its operator.

11. Further information

Obligation to provide data
You are not obliged to provide any data. Without a connection to a server, however, the app cannot be used; without the relevant permission, the related features are not available.
Automated decisions
There is no automated decision-making, including profiling, within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. Tiles, nudges and the AI assistant's answers are information for you, not decisions.
Children
The app is intended for students and learners. It is not specifically intended for children; the publisher does not knowingly collect data from children through the app.
Security
Sign-in uses passkeys; there are no passwords. How your own server is secured is the responsibility of its operator.
