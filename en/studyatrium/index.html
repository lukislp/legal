Privacy policy for the StudyAtrium app

Version 1.7 · Last updated: 20 November 2026

In short. StudyAtrium (formerly StudyLife) is an app for self-hosted server software for planning and tracking your studies. The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting software and sends your data only to the StudyAtrium server whose address you enter, and to Apple for notifications. The operator of that server, not the publisher of the app, is responsible for the data stored there. The publisher does not offer StudyAtrium as a service to others: he runs only a public, read-only demo and a review instance that is used solely for Apple's App Review and deleted afterwards. Data from Apple Health is evaluated on your device only and never leaves it.

3. The app on your device

3.1 Download from the App Store

The app is distributed through Apple's App Store. When you download it, Apple (Apple Distribution International Ltd., Ireland, or Apple Inc., USA) processes data such as your Apple ID, device data and purchase information under its own responsibility. The publisher has no influence on this. For details, see Apple's privacy policy.

3.2 What the app connects to

On first launch you enter the address of your StudyAtrium server. There is no default address. After that, the app connects only to

  • the StudyAtrium server whose address you entered, and
  • the Apple Push Notification service for notifications (section 3.4).

The app does not offer the marketplace for add-ons and therefore makes no connection to GitHub (section 3.10). The app contains no third-party software for audience measurement, advertising, tracking or crash reporting and uses neither Firebase nor any comparable service. Links, for example to the self-hosting guide or to "Report a problem" (a prefilled form on GitHub with the app version, platform, browser family and language), open in your browser; nothing is sent until you submit the form there yourself, and then GitHub's privacy terms apply.

You sign in with a passkey or, if the operator allows it, with a username and password. Only the passkey sign-in runs on a sign-in page of your server that opens in a system window. You enter your username, password and, if needed, a recovery code on the app's own page (the embedded interface), and the app sends them to your server. The password is sent to your server and stored there only as a salted, deliberately slow hash (PBKDF2), never in plain text; the app does not store it. Confirmation with Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode is handled by the operating system; the app receives no biometric data.

3.4 Notifications (push and local)

The app asks for permission to send you notifications. Reminders that the app schedules itself are created on the device. For notifications from your server, the app receives a device token from Apple and sends it, with the device name iOS gives the app (usually the model name such as "iPhone"), to your StudyAtrium server, which stores both. Your server sends notifications and Live Activity updates through the Apple Push Notification service (APNs) of Apple Inc. Apple processes the device token and the content.

The content can include reminders, reports, nudges and achievements, including course names and topics; Live Activity updates contain the state of the study timer (phase, round, end time). The server's operator is responsible for the processing on the server (section 5). When you sign out in the app, the app first asks your server to delete the device token and the Live Activity token (see below); this is attempted for a few seconds only and does not happen if the server cannot be reached or the session has already expired. Servers of the current version additionally delete the device tokens bound to your session themselves: when you sign out, when the session is ended (for example by revoking it or by recovery), when it expires, after 180 days without an active session, and when an account holds more than 20 registrations (the oldest is then replaced). A token that was registered before the server was updated, or on an older server version, and that was not deleted stays on the server until Apple reports it as invalid or your account is deleted. Only a server whose operator has set up an Apple push key for this app can send notifications through Apple; details are on the support page. A separate token for Live Activity updates is stored by the server with the state of your study timer. The app asks the server to delete it when the study timer's Live Activity ends and when you sign out, and the server deletes it when Apple reports it as expired. This token is not tied to your session: if these requests do not arrive (for example without a connection), it stays until it is replaced or your account is deleted. You can turn notifications off at any time in the system settings.

3.5 Apple Health

When you tap "Connect Apple Health" under "Setup" in the app, iOS asks which data you want to share. Before that, the app does not access Apple Health. The app

  • reads heart rate variability (for a readiness tile: the app looks at the last 30 days and shows the tile once at least 14 values are available), sleep analysis (sleep regularity over 30 nights), step count (a movement break nudge after about 25 minutes of focus) and VO2 max (cardio fitness trend in the statistics, over the last 365 days), and
  • writes one "Mindful Minutes" entry with the start and end of the focus time for each completed focus round.

The values read are evaluated and shown on your device only. They are not sent to your server, to the publisher or to any third party. Telemetry (section 3.8) contains no values from Apple Health and no information about which data types you share or whether access was granted. If you have agreed to telemetry, the app at most reports to your server that a technical query of the Health interface ended with success or with an error. Apple Health stores the Mindful Minutes written; whether they are synced via iCloud depends on your settings with Apple.

Health data enjoys special protection. Insofar as this involves processing of personal data by the publisher, it is based on your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a) and Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG), which you give by granting access in iOS. You can withdraw it at any time, entirely or for individual data types, in the Health app or in Settings under "Privacy & Security → Health". Apple Health is available on iPhone and on iPad with iPadOS 17 or later, not in the Mac app and not in the Apple Watch app; the values stay on the device on which you granted access.

3.6 Dictating notes

To dictate notes, the app records with the microphone and sends the recording to your StudyAtrium server. The server software converts it to text there with a speech recognition model (Whisper) that runs on the server itself; the recording is not stored and not sent to third parties. The operator of your server is responsible for this. If recording is not possible, the app falls back to Apple's speech recognition; Apple then processes the spoken content, depending on device, language and settings, on the device or on Apple's servers under its own responsibility. The microphone is used only while you have actively started a dictation.

3.7 Widgets, Live Activity, Apple Watch, shortcuts and calendar files

Widgets, the study timer's Live Activity, the Apple Watch app and the shortcuts (open the focus timer, ask for the streak, ask for today's or this week's study time, pause or resume the timer) use the summary from section 3.3. These extensions do not open network connections themselves. Pausing or resuming the timer and the quick actions on the app icon are carried out by the app itself (in the background if necessary), which sends the command to your server. If you open a calendar file (.ics) with StudyAtrium, the app reads the file to add the events to your StudyAtrium installation. In the background, the app occasionally refreshes its cache from your server.

3.8 Telemetry (only with consent, to your server's operator)

On first launch, StudyAtrium asks once whether you want to send technical measurements to improve errors and loading times. Without your agreement nothing is sent. With your agreement, the app sends to the operator of your StudyAtrium server (yourself if you run it yourself; to the publisher only if the publisher runs that server): loading times, response times and status codes of requests together with the route of the request (a template without IDs, for example api/sessions/{id}) and the HTTP method; the names of the pages you open (the first part of the page address, for example the statistics page) with their render time, which shows which parts of the app you use; web performance measures (Web Vitals) and start-up and resource timings; events of the live connection to your server; whether a push notification was registered or received; the app's memory, data and processor usage; error kinds with a cleaned, shortened error location (without the error text); the success or error of technical queries of the Health interface (no Health values or details, section 3.5); and crash and hang reports that iOS provides to the app via MetricKit. Each transmission also states the platform, the app version, the interface language and the type of connection (for example Wi-Fi or mobile). The events carry a random session ID that is replaced at the next start of the app or page load after 24 hours, but no user ID, no IP address and no content. Until you agree, the app stores neither the session ID nor any events on the device and sends nothing. Only the embedded interface already reads timing marks of the page start before you decide and keeps technical events (and a session ID created in memory) in the memory of the running view; they are not stored and are not sent without your consent. If you decline or withdraw consent, the app deletes the session ID and stored events. Your server stores your current decision in your settings; it is set only by a request from your signed-in app session, not by an access key. As proof of your consent (Art. 7(1) GDPR), the server also keeps a consent log to which each answer (consent or withdrawal) is only appended: the answer, the time (UTC) and the version of the consent text (currently version 2). The log contains no telemetry data, is kept until your account is deleted and is included in the data export. You can withdraw your consent at any time in Setup (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG); withdrawal takes effect for the future and ends the sending and storing of new data, and events already sent are not affected. The operator of your server is responsible for evaluating it.

5. Self-hosted StudyAtrium servers

The operator of the server you connect the app to is responsible for all data stored on that server – for example you yourself, a family member, a study group or an institution. Please contact that operator with questions about this data and to exercise your rights. They must provide you with their own privacy policy.

To help you understand what is typically processed there, the overview below describes the features of the software. What a particular server actually stores, for how long and with whom it shares data is decided by its operator.

  • Account: display name and passkeys (public key, device label, timestamps); if you set a password, your username and a hash of the password (not the password itself) with timestamps and a count of failed sign-in attempts; no email address. Sessions are stored as a hash with timestamps.
  • Content: study sessions (course, times, topic, note), notes, courses and programmes, goals per course including optional grades, settings (including your telemetry and AI choices), the telemetry consent log (section 3.8) and timer state. If you wish, you can create a public, read-only progress link (ECTS, grade average, topic progress and the names, semesters and ECTS of your active courses).
  • Notification subscriptions: for the app, Apple's device token with the device name (section 3.4); for browser notifications, your browser's push address and keys with its browser identifier. Browser notifications go through your browser's push service (for example Google, Mozilla, Apple or Microsoft).
  • Logs: the software does not otherwise log IP addresses. One exception: if a request carries forwarding headers (X-Forwarded-For or X-Forwarded-Proto) from a sender that the operator has not configured as a trusted proxy, the server ignores the headers and writes a warning with the IP address of that sender to its log, once per address and per start of the server (for at most 16 different addresses). Behind a reverse proxy that is not yet configured, this is the proxy's address; on a server exposed directly to the internet, it can be the address of a visitor or a scanner. How long this log is kept is decided by the operator. For protective limits, the IP address is kept only briefly (at most 15 minutes) in the server's memory or cache. What a web server in front of it logs is decided by the operator.
  • Add-ons are set up by the operator, and you turn each of them on yourself. The server then sends data to the respective service at your request: the Telegram bot to Telegram (Telegram FZ-LLC, United Arab Emirates), the Discord bot to Discord (Discord Inc., USA), the Alexa skill to Amazon (Amazon Europe Core S.à r.l., Luxembourg; Amazon.com, Inc., USA), the MCP interface to the AI assistant you connect it to (for example Claude by Anthropic, PBC, USA), webhooks to the addresses you enter (with your numeric account ID in the delivery), and Home Assistant, browser extensions and other tools to your own devices. These transfers can contain your content, such as course names, study times and notes. These services process the data under their own responsibility and privacy terms. The operator of the server, not the publisher, is responsible for setting them up and for the transfer; depending on the setup, the operator uses further service providers, for example for a tunnel or for backups. For AI, see section 8.
  • Retention: the software keeps your account and content until you delete your account; inactive accounts are not deleted automatically. Sign-in sessions expire after 90 days without use and after 180 days at the latest.
  • Export: every person can download their data in Setup as a machine-readable file (JSON): study sessions, notes, courses, programmes and groups, goals including grades, resources, templates and settings, and the telemetry consent log (answer, time and text version of each decision), plus a block with your account details and a block that describes the export itself.

5.1 Deleting your account

You can delete your account directly in the app under "Setup". For security you confirm the deletion with your passkey, your password or a recovery code (in the app also by a sign-in no more than two minutes old); an export is offered first. The server then deletes your account with all content, passkeys, password, sessions, keys, consents (including the consent log) and notification subscriptions. It also instructs every add-on that is connected to the server at that time to delete the data stored for your account: the AI assistant (search index, conversations and access), the MCP interface, the Alexa skill (links, access keys and tokens; if you revoked the add-on's access key beforehand, the instruction does not reach the skill's data: what remains is the encrypted, no longer valid key, the address of your server and hashes of the issued tokens, and the refresh token can be renewed for another 90 days each time it is used, so you ask your server's operator to remove this data), the Telegram bot (the link to your chat; if you revoked the add-on's access key beforehand, the instruction may not reach the link; the bot then normally removes it itself at its next request to your server, usually within about five minutes, otherwise you ask your server's operator to remove it), webhooks (targets and signing keys) and the developer portal (keys). The Discord bot stores no data about your account, so there is nothing to delete there. If an add-on cannot be reached at that moment, the server repeats the request later. Content that has already reached a third-party service, such as messages in your Telegram chat or Discord channel, your voice history at Amazon or conversations in your AI assistant, please delete there. Copies in the operator's backups remain until they expire; details are in your operator's privacy policy. For the data on your device, see section 3.3.

6.2 What the demo stores about you

These sessions are deleted at the regular reset of the demo, which takes place at least once a week and rebuilds all demo data. In your browser or in the app, the demo stores the session key, caches, the language, a marker of your user ID, the cached add-on overview and the choice of focus sound in local storage (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG); it sets no cookies. Because the demo never shows the telemetry question, the interface may keep technical events in the memory of the open page for the duration of your visit; they are never sent and disappear when you close the page. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (see above).

8. Optional AI features

The operator of a StudyAtrium server can set up an AI assistant (the "studylife-ai" add-on) that answers questions about your study content, enriches notes and carries out tasks. Which models are used is decided by the operator alone.

  • Data sent: your notes (title and content), courses (name, code, semester, ECTS, group, topics), study sessions of the last five years (course, times, status, topic, note) and goals (target date, grade, completion date and topics, completion note, tag). If you capture a web page, its title, text and address are also sent to the AI service for enrichment. This content is regularly transferred to the AI service and converted into vectors there with an embedding model; the service also stores the text excerpts and titles themselves next to the vectors. With every question, your message and matching excerpts go to the language model. Answers that list your content (for example a list of notes) additionally read note titles, a preview of 200 characters, course names and dates live from your server. Further models, for example for ranking excerpts, interpreting dates or recognising list requests, receive the question and excerpts as well and can be different models from the language model.
  • Possible recipients: the provider chosen by the operator, for example OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral, Microsoft Azure or another provider connected via LiteLLM, or a locally run model (for example Ollama), in which case no data goes to third parties.
  • Consent: before any data goes to an external provider, StudyAtrium shows you the provider, the models and the data shared, and asks for your explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). The consent applies only to these providers and models; if the operator changes them, you are asked again. Your server stores the time of your consent and an identifier of the providers and models it covers. Consent is only not required if the operator declares that all models run locally.
